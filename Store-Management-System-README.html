## 1. Executive Summary

The product supports a multi-store electronics and furniture retailer that purchases from distributors, sells for cash or store-provided credit, delivers every sold product, collects installments, transfers stock between stores, and may recover, repair, resell, or scrap repossessed items.

The solution has two clients over one shared backend and database:

- **Management System (web):** authoritative business administration, master data, approvals, sales, procurement, inventory, finance, HR, configuration, and detailed reporting.
- **Worker App (mobile):** role-specific operational execution, tasks, notifications, field work, customer interaction, evidence capture, personal records, and lightweight operational views.

The architecture uses a modular monolith on Supabase/PostgreSQL. Business rules, authorization, numbering, task generation, notification generation, and workflow transitions belong on the backend. Files live in object storage with metadata in PostgreSQL.

### Guiding principles

1. One shared source of truth. The Worker App is not a separate business database.
2. Business records explain **why**; movement ledgers record **what changed physically**.
3. Digital records are authoritative, but physical signed documents remain where operationally necessary.
4. Historical truth is preserved. Finalized transactions, payroll, approvals, prices, addresses, and physical-unit histories must not be rewritten by later configuration changes.
5. Human judgment remains central for credit, pricing exceptions, repossession, inspection, repair, resale, and exceptional returns.
6. No arbitrary deletion of customers, employees, products, stock units, or official documents.
7. Search, attachments, document generation, permissions, notifications, and traceability are cross-cutting capabilities.
8. Detailed schemas may evolve, but the business invariants in this document must be preserved.

## 2. Business Scope

### 2.1 Retail model

- Multiple stores operate under one company.
- Stores generally purchase independently, though they may use common suppliers.
- The business is demand-driven but also operates showrooms and may hold showroom/storage stock.
- Catalog products may exist even when a store has zero physical units.
- Every sold electronics or furniture item is delivered by the store in the normal workflow.
- Cash/COD and installment credit are supported.
- Inventory can move within a store and between stores.
- Repossessed/recovered products can be inspected, repaired, resold, or disposed of.
- Legal proceedings after a refused repossession are outside system scope, though attempts and outcomes remain recorded.

### 2.2 Decision-support philosophy

The system provides data, history, warnings, workflows, approvals, and auditability. It must not blindly automate decisions that depend on management judgment, including:

- credit approval and reassessment;
- price exceptions;
- default and repossession decisions;
- recovered-product disposition;
- repair viability;
- resale classification and price;
- exceptional complaint or return resolution.

## 3. Application and Infrastructure Architecture

### 3.1 Applications

#### Management System

Optimized for office/PC use and information-heavy workflows. Primary users include Owner, Manager, Admin, and other authorized finance/administrative roles. It manages:

- company/store reference data;
- employees, roles, permissions, and reporting relationships;
- customers and documents;
- products, variants, prices, and stock;
- sales orders, surveys, approvals, and fulfillment;
- purchase orders, receiving, suppliers, and supplier payments;
- receivables, payments, expenses, budgets, payroll, and reports;
- task, notification, bulletin, document, and configuration administration.

#### Worker App

Optimized for mobile operational work. It is not a miniature copy of the Management System. Each role sees only relevant work, documents, context, records, and decisions.

Core areas:

- My Work and calendar;
- operational data and documents;
- customer/work context;
- personal attendance, leave, payroll, targets, and commission;
- performance views;
- notifications;
- bulletin/reference information;
- notes, photographs, attachments, and evidence;
- search, history, account/profile, and poor-connection considerations.

Freelance salespeople do not use the Worker App and do not receive normal employee salary/benefits. They are commission-only external workers managed by the sales SPV.

### 3.2 Shared backend

- Both clients use one backend and one PostgreSQL database.
- Supabase provides PostgreSQL, authentication, object storage, realtime capabilities, and server-side/edge capabilities.
- Application-defined backend logic remains authoritative.
- Important validation cannot exist only in UI code.
- Architecture begins as a **modular monolith**, not microservices.

Suggested modules:

`auth`, `store`, `employee`, `customer`, `product`, `pricing`, `sales`, `survey`, `inventory`, `procurement`, `delivery`, `finance`, `compensation`, `task`, `notification`, `document`, `reporting`, `approval`.

### 3.3 Event-driven workflow

Business events trigger centralized rules and resulting actions. Examples:

- official SO released → number assigned → reservation evaluated → survey/task generated → recipients notified;
- delivery assignment → worker task and persistent notification;
- collection attempt completed → task closed → follow-up generated when necessary;
- approved receiving → stock units/component quantities created → stock movements posted;
- approved stock adjustment → movements posted, never direct silent quantity overwrites.

### 3.4 Authentication and authorization

- Authentication identifies the account.
- Authorization determines actions and data scope.
- Enforcement occurs at backend/database level, not only through hidden UI controls.
- Role/position supplies baseline permissions.
- Menu/action permission and data scope are separate concerns.
- Detailed exceptions can be added only when an actual business need appears.

The latest technical direction supports reusable approval configuration:

- `approval_workflow`
- `approval_workflow_step`
- `approval_request`
- `approval_request_step`

A request snapshots its actual steps so later workflow changes do not rewrite historical approvals. A generic `reference_type + reference_id` may connect approval requests to business objects.

## 4. Organization, Employees, and Accounts

### 4.1 Core rules

- One company contains multiple stores. A separate multi-organization layer is not currently required.
- Every employee has a permanent employee number that does not encode role or store.
- Employees are never deleted; they become inactive.
- Employee/person data, employment data, and application-account data are distinct concepts.
- An employee may exist without an application login.
- Employee creation occurs in the Management System by Owner, Manager, or Admin.
- Regular employees cannot create their own employee master record.

### 4.2 Employee information

Personal data may include name, birth information, NIK/KTP, identification documents, contacts, address, and emergency contact. Employment data may include employee number, role, store, reporting supervisor, employment type/status, dates, and assignments.

Historical/transactional records are not overwritten biodata:

- attendance;
- leave;
- tasks/work history;
- targets and performance;
- commission;
- payroll.

### 4.3 Current assignment versus historical context

Earlier discussions requested historical employee-store/SPV relationships. Later technical discussion proposed simpler current tables without generic employee history tables. The resolved rule is:

- Keep the current assignment and reporting structure in active relationship records.
- Preserve event-time store, role, salesperson, SPV, approver, and related context on business records where historical reporting requires it.
- Do not create broad history tables without a concrete query/use case.
- Never allow present-day reassignment to change historical transaction attribution.

Possible current structures:

- `employee`
- `role`
- `employee_role`
- `employee_store`
- `employee_reporting`
- `permission`
- `role_permission`

### 4.4 Attendance, leave, and payroll display

- Attendance originates from a fingerprint/biometric device integration to be designed later.
- Employees view attendance in the Worker App.
- Owner, Manager, or Admin may correct attendance; corrections retain actor/time evidence.
- Employees cannot directly alter attendance.
- Leave requests include type, dates, reason, status, balance, and history.
- Approval follows reporting authority; the actual approver is stored.
- Payroll is calculated and finalized in the Management System; Worker App only displays the frozen result and breakdown.

## 5. Customer Domain

### 5.1 Customer identity

- One company-wide Customer master is used across all stores.
- There is no separate Prospect entity. A customer record may exist before a completed purchase.
- No manually maintained Active/Inactive customer flag is required.
- Customer records are permanent and cannot be deleted.
- NIK is the authoritative unique identity where available/required; name and phone remain search keys.
- Search for an existing customer before creating a new record during the SO workflow.
- No permanent customer ownership by a salesperson. The meaningful attribution is Customer → SO → salesperson/store.

### 5.2 Creation and maintenance

Earlier data design said Customer Master is automatically created or linked when an SO is created and not through a standalone create menu. Later technical design clarifies that customer updates are controlled through SO/workflow rather than free editing from the Customer menu. Resolved behavior:

1. During Draft SO, search by NIK and other identifiers.
2. Link an existing customer when found.
3. If not found, create the customer as part of completing/releasing the SO workflow.
4. Update only allowed customer fields through the relevant workflow, with validation and traceability.

### 5.3 Contacts, addresses, and documents

- One customer may have multiple contacts and multiple addresses.
- Suggested tables: `customer_contact`, `customer_address`, `customer_document`.
- Files such as KTP/KK/supporting evidence live in object storage; metadata and references live in PostgreSQL.
- An SO stores a snapshot of the delivery address used for that transaction. Later customer-address changes cannot alter old SOs or deliveries.
- Structured family relationships are not currently required.

### 5.4 History

The customer view should expose linked SOs, surveys, deliveries, receivables, payments, collection attempts, repossessions, warranty/complaint records, cancellations, and documents. A customer can have unlimited SOs, multiple concurrent credit obligations, and purchases across stores.

## 6. Product, Variant, Components, and Stock

### 6.1 Product hierarchy

Latest model:

- **Product:** catalog/family definition, permanent and non-deletable.
- **Product Variant:** specific sellable configuration and stock pool.
- **Stock Unit:** individually tracked physical sellable item.
- **Product Component:** standard content expected with a product.
- **Component Stock:** loose/spare component inventory held in quantity.

Examples of variants include size/color combinations. A Stock Unit has a permanent internal ID; optional manufacturer serial/IMEI remains separate.

### 6.2 Attached versus loose components

- A remote, cable, stand, or base expected with a product is represented in the product definition.
- Each Stock Unit can record component availability/completeness.
- Components already attached to a Stock Unit are not separate Component Stock.
- Loose replacement components are maintained as Component Stock quantities.
- Installing a spare component consumes Component Stock and makes it part of the Stock Unit.
- No separate `repair_part` table is required for now; the repair event and stock movement capture consumption.

### 6.3 Stock locations and state

Latest location baseline:

- Each store has logical locations such as Warehouse, Showroom, and Repair Area.
- No shelf/rack/bin tracking.
- A store does not currently require multiple warehouses/showrooms.
- Inventory can be outside the store, in transit, at a customer, supplier, or repair provider.
- External exact addresses do not need to be modeled as store locations; the related business record can identify the holder/provider.
- Store current location/state can be denormalized on Stock Unit for performance while Stock Movement remains historical authority.

Stock Unit state must distinguish at least:

- current store/location or outside/in-transit state;
- availability/allocation state;
- current operational status;
- permanent movement/history.

The exact status enum remains an implementation decision. Avoid one overloaded status when separate location, availability, repair, warranty, and business records are clearer.

### 6.4 Permanent history

A Stock Unit is never deleted. Possible lifecycle:

`Received → Warehouse → Showroom → Reserved → Allocated → Delivered → Customer → Repossessed → Inspected → Repaired → Resold → Disposed`

Leaving active inventory does not remove historical searchability.

### 6.5 Inventory ledger

The latest design makes `stock_movement` the centralized inventory ledger. Every physical quantity, location, or unit-state change produces a movement. The source business document explains why.

Examples:

- receiving;
- transfer in/out;
- delivery;
- repair dispatch/return and component consumption;
- warranty movement;
- repossession/stock return;
- scrap/disposal;
- approved adjustment.

Do not reconstruct history by UNION-ing all transaction tables. Current cached quantities may exist for performance, but ledger consistency must be transactional.

## 7. Reservation, Allocation, and Fulfillment

These concepts are distinct:

- **Reservation:** temporary lock or commitment.
- **Allocation:** permanent association between an SO item and exact physical Stock Units.
- **Delivery Item:** records which allocated units were actually delivered.
- **Stock Movement:** records the resulting physical inventory change.

### 7.1 Latest reservation trigger

The source discussions changed several times: verbal commitment, released SO, and finally a narrower technical trigger. The newest rule in file 4 wins:

- A normal Draft SO does not reserve stock.
- Stock is reserved when the SO reaches **Waiting for Owner Price Approval**.
- This prevents dummy drafts from locking desirable stock.
- For non-negotiated orders, the implementation must define the equivalent meaningful reservation boundary during release. It must not contradict the invariant that drafts alone do not reserve.
- No automatic time-based expiry is currently required.
- Rejection/cancellation/invalidity releases reservations.
- One exact Stock Unit may have only one active reservation, ideally enforced by a partial unique index.

### 7.2 Quantity and exact-unit handling

- One SO item may represent multiple quantities of one variant.
- Exact physical fulfillment uses a junction such as `so_item_stock_unit`.
- Active reservation count cannot exceed required quantity.
- A damaged/unusable allocation can be replaced by another unit without rewriting history.
- Component Stock reservation mechanics remain deferred.

## 8. Sales Order and Pricing

### 8.1 Draft and release

- Draft SO is a temporary salesperson workspace for gradually collected information.
- Drafts have no official document number, do not appear as sales, do not create survey tasks, and normally do not reserve stock.
- Release validates completeness.
- If price negotiation is required, owner/authorized approval occurs before official release.
- Rejected price negotiation returns to revision and does not create an official SO.
- Official number is assigned only at release.
- Abandoned draft retention/purge duration remains open.

### 8.2 Official lifecycle

`Draft → Validate → Price Approval if required → Release → Survey → Pending Confirmation → Customer Confirmation + required DP → Fulfillment → Delivery → Payment/Installments → Completed`

Rules:

- Rejection differs from cancellation.
- Creator may cancel before survey and during Pending Confirmation.
- Pending Confirmation is the last simple cancellation point.
- After confirmation and required DP, cancellation requires controlled exceptional handling.
- Delivered does not mean Completed.
- The SO completes only after required delivery and financial settlement.
- SO items can progress independently and can be partially delivered.

### 8.3 Pricing

Latest pricing model overrides older fixed-price-list formula discussions:

- Prices are store-specific, not global.
- Every item may support cash and configured credit/installment plans.
- Normal terms may include 10, 12, and 15 months; cheaper items may support 3 or 6 months. Terms are configurable.
- Installment total prices are configured values for now, not automatically calculated by a universal formula.
- Suggested structures: `store_product_price` and `store_product_payment_plan`.
- No permanent selling price is stored on every Stock Unit.
- SO Item stores selected plan, reference price, and final agreed transaction unit price.
- Changing master price later never changes an existing SO.
- Negotiation uses the generic approval workflow; a standalone price-negotiation table is not currently required.
- Delivery fee and other charges remain separate from product price.
- Promotions are deferred but should not be architecturally blocked.
- Older statements about versioned price lists/effective ranges are superseded by the newer decision not to add price history/effective-to until a proven need appears. Historical SO prices remain preserved through transaction snapshots.

## 9. Survey and Credit Assessment

### 9.1 Business purpose

- Credit purchases require customer assessment; cash purchases do not require credit survey.
- Salesperson collects and verifies complete customer information before release.
- Surveyor performs physical verification and structured assessment using appropriate customer/order information.
- Customer income, expenses, obligations, residence, work/business, references, and ability to pay may be captured.
- GPS/photos/evidence may be captured; detailed map/evidence implementation remains open.
- Previous defaults/repossessions generate warnings but do not automatically block credit.
- No fixed customer credit limit; approval is purchase-specific.

### 9.2 Latest result/relationship model

- Survey is linked to the SO, not directly to customer master.
- Latest design uses `1 SO → 1 Survey → 1 Surveyor`.
- Result is Approved or Rejected.
- Rejected Survey makes the SO Rejected.
- No resurvey loop or separate survey history/result table is planned unless a real requirement emerges.
- Earlier descriptions gave final approval to a Manager, while later Worker App and technical discussions made the Surveyor's Approved/Rejected result the sales approval. The later rule wins: the surveyor records the decisive survey result; price approval remains separate.
- Exact Survey locking/edit boundary remains open, but finalized/advanced transactions must preserve history.

## 10. Procurement, Supplier, and Receiving

### 10.1 Supplier and PO

- Supplier has multiple contacts.
- PO belongs to exactly one store and one supplier, and contains multiple items.
- PO may fulfill one or more SOs or, rarely, general stock/showroom needs.
- Admin normally creates PO; Admin, Manager, or Owner may create it.
- Manager approval is normally required, subject to configurable approval workflow.
- Purchase cost lives on PO/PO Item as transaction data, not Product master data.
- Procurement of loose spare components/equipment uses PO, not Operational Expense.

### 10.2 Receiving

- One PO can have multiple receiving documents and partial receipts.
- Receiving occurs at the PO's store.
- Every incoming product/component is inspected before inventory acceptance.
- Incorrect, damaged, or unacceptable items are rejected to supplier and do not become inventory.
- Partial acceptance is supported.
- A Stock Unit ID is created/applied after acceptance; manufacturer serial is recorded where available.
- Receiving inspection uses configurable checklists editable by authorized Manager/Owner.
- Approved receiving creates the appropriate Stock Movements.

### 10.3 Supplier payments

- Receiving and supplier-payment status are independent.
- Earlier requirements included supplier-level collection and giro/check clearing. Later technical design simplified `supplier_payment`, said supplier payments are cash, and declined bank-account tracking. Apply the later implementation direction unless the real process reconfirms giro.
- At minimum retain supplier, PO/payable context, amount, date, payer, method/status, and notes.
- If giro/check is used in production, Paid must occur only after clearing; failed clearing must restore/retain outstanding balance.

## 11. Delivery and Logistics

### 11.1 Delivery business process

- Store handles normal delivery of all sold items.
- Driver plus optional Helper performs transport; one person is allowed when safe.
- Actual destination may differ from customer residence and is snapshotted on the SO/delivery.
- Before departure, responsible staff and delivery team verify product condition and completeness.
- Delivery includes carrying, setup/installation of the purchased item where applicable, and basic functional verification, not unrelated services.
- Customer inspects and signs physical confirmation on acceptance.
- Problems before signature are delivery issues, not warranty claims.
- Delivery damage is investigated and resolved by the store.
- Failed attempts are recorded and rescheduled; exact allocation remains.
- GPS is supporting data, not definitive proof of correct delivery.

### 11.2 Data model

- `1 SO → many Deliveries`.
- Each Delivery contains Delivery Items linked to allocated `so_item_stock_unit` records.
- Partial delivery and multiple batches are supported.
- Delivery status is independent of SO status.
- Only successful delivery causes the physical stock movement out of active inventory.
- No redundant `delivery_stock_unit` is needed when Delivery Item resolves through the allocation.

### 11.3 Picking/trip

Earlier design defined one Picking/Trip for many Outbound documents and multiple customer stops. Preserve the operational capability even if final table names evolve:

- Manager assigns one driver per trip and optional helpers.
- One trip may carry multiple customer deliveries/outbounds.
- Driver sees Picking/Delivery Note data, destinations, customer/product details, COD, and item-level statuses.
- Driver records success, rejection/failure reason, notes, optional evidence, and partial item results.
- After return, reconcile delivered/returned goods, signed documents, COD custody, and issues before trip closure.

## 12. Receivables, Payments, Collection, and Custody

### 12.1 Financial model

Latest direction:

- `receivable` represents total customer obligation for an SO.
- `receivable_installment` represents the schedule.
- `payment` records money received.
- `payment_allocation` maps one payment across one or more installments, including partial amounts.
- Cached paid/outstanding amounts may exist for performance but allocations are historical truth.
- Partial payment, overdue amount, promises, and payment history must remain representable.

### 12.2 Allocation

Older baseline said unspecified payments allocate oldest outstanding installment first and excess becomes customer credit. Preserve this as the default business rule unless later explicitly changed. Payment allocation must support one payment across multiple installments.

### 12.3 Collection attempts

The latest technical design says collection is not a separate financial object. However, the business and Worker App explicitly require field visits even when no payment occurs. Resolved model:

- Financial collection is Payment against Receivable.
- Operational visit/attempt is a distinct task/attempt record when the business needs route, outcome, promise date, reason, notes, and repeat-visit history.
- A visit is complete when its outcome is recorded, whether paid, partial, unavailable, postponed, or no payment.
- Original due date is not changed by a promised payment date.
- Failed/unpaid attempts may create future tasks without overwriting the old attempt.
- Any suitable assigned employee may perform the visit; no permanent collector ownership/territory.

### 12.4 Cash custody

- Customer payment can be received by a collector/staff member and later handed to Admin/Cashier.
- `payment_custody` or equivalent records current responsible holder, timestamps, handover status, and discrepancies.
- Transfer payments do not require physical cash handover but still need payment confirmation.
- Cash handover discrepancy is recorded; offline business resolution does not erase system evidence.
- Avoid building a full universal cash-account ledger unless future accounting scope requires it.

### 12.5 Overdue and default

- Due dates and overdue duration are tracked.
- No late fee, automatic penalty, default, or repossession solely from elapsed days.
- Warning and contact attempts are retained.
- Manager decides whether to continue collection, grant time, or initiate repossession.

## 13. Repossession, Return, Repair, Warranty, and Disposal

### 13.1 Repossession

- Manager-controlled after collection/warning; never automatic.
- Driver plus optional Helper performs recovery.
- Attempt results, refusal, assigned staff, dates, reason, approval, and notes are captured.
- On successful repossession: remaining balance is cancelled/forgiven, prior payments are retained, payment history remains, and obligation is marked settled through repossession rather than normally paid.
- Original Stock Unit ID remains.
- Returned item is Unavailable until inspection and disposition.
- Prior repossession warns future assessment but does not prohibit credit.

Latest technical design may combine physical return/recovery into `stock_return` with type such as `CUSTOMER_RETURN` or `REPOSSESSION`. This table is the inbound physical event; the business reason and financial outcome must remain distinguishable.

### 13.2 Inspection and recovered-product disposition

Product-specific inspection determines:

1. sell as-is;
2. external repair then reinspection/resale;
3. disposal/scrap, possibly with sale proceeds.

No universal depreciation, condition, or resale-price formula. Authorized staff sets the actual transaction price. Preserve original cost, payments, forgiven amount, repair cost, scrap proceeds, and resale price for later analysis.

### 13.3 Repair

- Repair is an event involving a Stock Unit.
- Track problem, diagnosis, action, provider, cost, sent/completed dates, status, and history.
- Repairs are external in the current baseline.
- Under-repair inventory cannot be sold.
- Spare-component use consumes Component Stock through Stock Movement.

### 13.4 Warranty and complaints

- Complaint, warranty claim, delivery rejection, and exceptional return are distinct meanings.
- Problems found before customer acceptance/signature remain incomplete delivery issues.
- Post-acceptance manufacturing-defect cases use Warranty Claim.
- Store coordinates the relationship and verifies purchase/warranty context.
- Manufacturer/distributor owns the technical coverage decision.
- Outcomes: repair same unit, replacement with same model/different physical unit, or rejection.
- Valid warranty cost is borne by manufacturer/distributor.
- Customer-caused damage may lead to rejected warranty and optional paid repair.
- Warranty terms vary by product/brand/distributor and are not globally hardcoded.
- Replacement physical unit receives its own Stock Unit ID and links to the replaced unit.
- Product custody/movement is tracked between customer, store, and provider.
- No generic change-of-mind return program.
- Temporary loaner replacement remains optional/future scope.

### 13.5 Disposal

- `stock_disposal` and items represent permanent inventory exit.
- Scrap sale can record buyer, proceeds, date, and reason.
- Historical Stock Unit remains searchable.

## 14. Stock Transfer, Stock Count, and Adjustment

### 14.1 Transfer

One Stock Transfer mechanism supports:

- inter-store transfer, such as Store A Warehouse → Store B Warehouse;
- intra-store transfer, such as Warehouse → Showroom.

Track source/destination store and location, items, requester, approval, dispatch, in-transit state, receipt, verification, and completion. Different transfer types may have different approval requirements. Reserved stock cannot be transferred between stores unless reservation is validly released/reassigned.

### 14.2 Stock opname

Latest frequency decision overrides an earlier month-end rule:

- No fixed stock-opname schedule.
- Management initiates it when necessary.
- Manager and bookkeeping/accounting staff commonly perform it.
- It compares expected exact units/quantities to physical reality.
- It flags discrepancies but does not directly redefine inventory.

### 14.3 Adjustment

- Discrepancy → investigation → decision → authorized adjustment.
- No casual generic stock-edit button.
- `stock_adjustment` and items provide reason, actor, approver, affected unit/component/location, quantity/type, notes, and timestamps.
- Approval posts Stock Movements; it never silently overwrites quantity/history.
- Possible outcomes include correcting a recording error, confirming loss, marking damaged/display state, or disposing/scrapping.
- Individual Stock Units change state/location instead of using a quantity field.

## 15. Expenses, Budgets, Activities, and Supplier Money

### 15.1 Expense versus procurement

- Vendor purchases of products, spare components, or equipment use PO/Receiving.
- Utilities, internet, fuel, rent, canvassing spending, and other operational costs use Expense.
- Expense category is configurable.
- Every expense requires proof/receipt as attachment where applicable.
- Immediate expense and an outstanding bill are conceptually different, though final table design may evolve.

### 15.2 Budget requests and activities

Latest design separates planned authorization from actual spending:

- Budget Request = requested/approved amount and breakdown.
- Expense = actual amount spent.
- Activity = canvassing, training, promotion, store event, team activity, or other operational purpose.
- Activity can have leader, participants, dates, status, budget requests, and expenses.
- Budget overrun should route to appropriate approval, not prevent recording a necessary real-world expense.
- Generic Budget Request is preferred over a canvassing-specific budget object.

Earlier baseline described monthly store operational budgets. Retain this business capability if still operationally required, but implement through the general budget/approval model rather than a hardcoded canvassing or monthly-only workflow.

## 16. Compensation and Payroll

### 16.1 Sales period

Compensation does not use simple calendar months. The period ends on the Sunday of the week containing the target month's final calendar day; the next period starts Monday; payout is the first day after cutoff.

Examples documented for 2026:

- August: 3 Aug - 6 Sep; payout 7 Sep.
- September: 7 Sep - 4 Oct; payout 5 Oct.
- October: 5 Oct - 1 Nov; payout 2 Nov.
- November: 2 Nov - 6 Dec; payout 7 Dec.

Store explicit start, cutoff, and payout dates.

### 16.2 Sales recognition and commission

Latest compensation rule overrides older first-payment recognition:

- Confirmed SO date determines compensation period.
- Confirmation includes required customer confirmation and DP where applicable.
- Later repossession does not reverse already earned sales commission.
- SF commission is tier-based, not marginal: the reached tier's rate applies to the entire eligible amount.
- Rules, tier values, and rates are configurable and effective-period aware.

### 16.3 Generic compensation engine

Metrics may include individual confirmed sales, team confirmed sales, team manpower, and attendance. Components may include base salary, title allowance, BOP, commission, performance allowance, incentive, other allowances, and deductions.

Current design direction:

- `compensation_component`
- `compensation_metric`
- `compensation_rule`
- `sales_period`
- `payroll`
- `payroll_item`

Exact columns remain open. Payroll calculation stores/finalizes actual component results. Later rule changes cannot change historical payroll. Employees receive one payout with a detailed item breakdown.

### 16.4 BOP and deductions

- BOP may equal eligible attendance days × applicable daily BOP.
- Approved leave counts as eligible attendance.
- SPV BOP may additionally depend on manpower.
- Employee loans/advances and penalties are separate business processes that feed payroll as deductions.

## 17. Worker App Role Scope

### Owner

Business-wide and multi-store overview, exceptions, approvals, financial/receivable/store performance, HR visibility, notifications, personal records, and quick actions. Detailed reporting presentation remains partly open.

### Manager

Runs daily store operations: assignments, manual tasks where useful, calendar, team status, routine approvals, leave, budget/expense attention, deliveries, surveys, collections, inventory alerts, operational summary, and downloadable reports.

### SPV

Worker App only in current baseline. Manages assigned sales team, team tasks/calendar, customers/SOs/follow-ups, targets, achievements, leave, and relevant notifications. Cannot alter transaction finance, prices, formulas, payroll, receivables, suppliers, other teams, or company-wide finance.

### Salesperson/SF

Own customers in work context, inquiries/follow-ups, draft/released SO creation, negotiation request, product/stock visibility, survey status, sale status, target/progress, and estimated commission. Follow-up is a lightweight reminder and not an SO/document.

### Surveyor

Assigned survey task, customer/order/financial context, structured checklist, GPS/photos/evidence, Approved/Rejected result, and status history.

### Driver

Trips, destinations, delivery documents, customer/product quantities, item-level outcomes, failure reasons, COD custody, and trip reconciliation.

### Collector

Assigned visits, customer/contact/credit/history context, outcome recording, partial/full/no payment, promise dates, cash/transfer collection, and custody handover. Collector chooses visit order while management determines who needs visits.

### Admin

Office administration, bookkeeping support, paperwork, walk-in sales, financial recording, operational funds, supplier payment recording, relevant documents, notifications, and Management System workspace. No owner-only approval/configuration powers.

### AR

Receivables monitoring, due/overdue lists, payment history, promised dates, collection results, handovers, transfer-payment review, reconciliation, and discrepancies. AR does not manage field routes.

### Helper/OB

Minimal task-focused experience: delivery assistance, loading/unloading, warehouse preparation, supplier-delivery support, manual tasks, simple status/calendar, and employee self-service. No customer finance, sales performance, supplier finance, store finance, payroll of others, or management reports.

## 18. Tasks, Notifications, Bulletin, Documents, Search, and Evidence

### 18.1 Tasks

- Task = work an employee needs to perform.
- Status: Pending → In Progress → Complete, with role-specific failure/outcome details where required.
- System-generated or manually created.
- Optional source reference to SO, Survey, Delivery, Activity, etc.
- One current assignee.
- Reassignment allowed only before work begins.
- Manual tasks are optional, used where a digital record is useful rather than for every verbal instruction.

### 18.2 Notifications

- Important backend events create persistent notification records.
- Push is a delivery mechanism; in-app notification remains readable.
- Notifications may have multiple recipients and link to source records.
- Recipient selection follows event/role/data scope.
- Notifications are not the same as permanent reference content.

### 18.3 Bulletin

Bulletin is the reference center for office rules, commission/target information, SOPs, guidelines, policies, and persistent company information. Management System manages content; Worker App reads/searches it. A bulletin may also trigger a notification.

### 18.4 Documents and numbering

- Structured business record is authoritative; printable document is its representation.
- System-generated and uploaded documents link to their source.
- Physical signatures/customer copies remain where useful; signed scans/photos can be attached.
- Expense receipt is evidence attached to Expense, not necessarily a system-numbered generated document.
- Official/released documents receive permanent numbers; drafts do not.
- Number includes month/year, store, type, and a yearly increment, separated by `/`.
- Cancelled numbers are never reused.
- Exact code order/padding remains implementation detail.

### 18.5 Search and attachments

Search must cover customers, NIK, SO/document numbers, products/variants, Stock Unit ID, manufacturer serial, employees, payments, and other key records. Attachments include survey evidence, customer documents, signed confirmations, receipts, product images, and employee documents.

### 18.6 Audit/history resolution

An earlier requirement called for broad Audit Trail. Latest technical direction rejects a universal log of every change and a giant Business History table. Resolved approach:

- Domain entities retain their meaningful lifecycle, actors, timestamps, and status/history.
- Stock Movement, Payment Allocation/Custody, Approval Request steps, Payroll items, and document records act as domain evidence.
- Add a dedicated system audit log only for concrete accountability/security requirements.
- Sensitive corrections must still identify who changed what and when.

## 19. Reporting and Dashboards

### Dashboard

Answers: **What is happening and what needs attention?** It is summarized, role-scoped, interactive, and drill-down capable.

### Report

Answers: **Give me the detailed records.** Reports may be downloadable as PDF and, where appropriate, Excel/CSV.

### Management questions

The system should answer:

1. How much was sold?
2. How much cash was actually collected?
3. How much remains receivable?
4. Who missed due/promised payments?
5. Why were payments missed?
6. What stock exists, where, and in what state?
7. How much was purchased and remains open?
8. Are individual/team/store targets being met?
9. Which factors correlate with defaults?
10. Which operational exceptions need attention?

Views should distinguish sales, cash received, outstanding receivables, gross margin, and estimated profitability. Do not label an operational estimate as official accounting profit without full accounting rules.

Suggested areas:

- Executive/store overview;
- Sales;
- Collection/receivables;
- Inventory/movements;
- Purchasing/suppliers;
- Finance/expenses/budgets/custody;
- HR/attendance/payroll/leave/performance.

Worker App shows small operational summaries; detailed tables/reports should generally be generated/downloaded rather than rendered as huge mobile grids.

## 20. Resolved Conflict Register

The following older statements are superseded by newer file-4 decisions or explicitly reconciled:

1. **Reservation timing:** verbal commitment/released SO → latest rule: Draft does not reserve; reservation occurs at Waiting for Owner Price Approval, with release-equivalent handling needed for normal-price SOs.
2. **Survey approval:** earlier Manager final approval → later Surveyor Approved/Rejected is decisive; owner price approval remains separate.
3. **Survey multiplicity/resurvey:** earlier repeated visit/task distinction → latest database baseline uses one Survey per SO and no resurvey loop. Operational retry only if later required.
4. **Pricing formulas/history:** earlier generalized calculator and versioned price lists → latest configured store/payment-plan prices, no automatic formula and no master price history until needed; SO snapshots final price.
5. **Commission recognition:** earlier first payment → latest confirmed SO date.
6. **Commission reversal:** later repossession does not reverse earned commission.
7. **Stock opname frequency:** earlier monthly month-end → later no fixed schedule; management-triggered.
8. **Employee assignment history:** earlier explicit history → later avoid generic history tables; preserve event-time attribution and only add targeted history where retrieval is required.
9. **Audit model:** earlier broad audit trail → later domain-owned history plus optional targeted audit log.
10. **Collection table:** financial Collection object removed; keep operational collection attempt/visit only when needed, while Payment/Allocation/Custody handle money.
11. **Return/repossession tables:** later physical inventory design may use one typed Stock Return, while business meaning and financial treatment remain distinct.
12. **Supplier payment method:** earlier giro/check lifecycle versus later simplified cash supplier payment. Validate real current practice before implementation; if giro exists, clearing status is mandatory.
13. **Price terms:** older 6/12/15 and later 10/12/15, plus 3/6 for cheap products. Latest rule is configurable terms per store/product payment plan.
14. **Installment start:** older first-delivery contract date versus later per-item/receivable design. Implement schedules according to finalized receivable rules, preserving partial/multiple delivery support and transaction snapshots.
